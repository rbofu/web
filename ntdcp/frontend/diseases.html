Diseases We Address

Tanzania's NTD programme targets five priority diseases through integrated preventive chemotherapy, vector control, WASH collaboration, and case management, guided by World Health Organization roadmaps for elimination.

Vector-borne

Lymphatic Filariasis

A mosquito-transmitted parasitic infection that can cause severe swelling of the limbs (lymphoedema) and genitals (hydrocele) when left untreated. Tanzania delivers annual mass drug administration in endemic districts, combined with morbidity management for existing cases.

Water-borne

Schistosomiasis

Caused by parasitic worms transmitted through contact with contaminated freshwater. The programme runs school-based deworming, community treatment, and works with WASH partners to reduce transmission around lakes and irrigation schemes.

Soil-transmitted

Soil-Transmitted Helminths

Intestinal worm infections affecting nutrition and child development. Annual deworming reaches pre-school and school-age children nationwide through the education and health sectors jointly.

Blinding disease

Trachoma

The world's leading infectious cause of blindness. Addressed through the SAFE strategy — Surgery, Antibiotics, Facial cleanliness, and Environmental improvement — in affected regions.

Vector-borne

Onchocerciasis (River Blindness)

Transmitted by blackflies breeding near fast-flowing rivers. Community-directed treatment with ivermectin continues in endemic foci, alongside entomological monitoring.
